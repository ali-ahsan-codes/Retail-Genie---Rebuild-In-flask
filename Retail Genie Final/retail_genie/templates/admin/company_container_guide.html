THE TENANCY MODEL

Company vs. tenant container

Company is the operating/legal business. It has its own stores, team memberships, inventory, sales, customers, settings, and business history.

Tenant container is the SaaS isolation and billing boundary that holds one or more separate companies. Companies can share a container without becoming one company. Data sharing between sibling companies is controlled by the container's data policies; the default is company-level isolation.

Example: Company A and Company B can both reside in Container 1, while their company IDs, users, stores, and records remain distinct. A container move changes the container boundary, not the company's identity.

1 · ONBOARDING

Add a company

  1. Sign in as a Super Admin and open Companies.
  2. For a new customer boundary, use Create company. Enter the company name/code, timezone, first store name/code, and the first Company Admin's username and temporary password. This creates a new active tenant container, company, store, and initial administrator membership together.
  3. For another company in an existing container, choose Add related company on the parent company. The new company remains a separate entity under that parent, but is inside the same container.
  4. Confirm the company and store appear in the directory. Give the Company Admin the temporary password through your approved secure channel; they must change it at first sign-in.

Before adding a sibling company: review the container's data policies. Container-wide sharing can expose eligible shared data across siblings; do not change a policy unless the customer has approved that sharing scope.

2 · STORES & INVENTORY

Set up inventory

  1. From Companies, open the company and choose Stores to add each branch/store. Use a distinct store code within that company.
  2. Open the app's Products / Inventory workspace. Create or import the approved product catalog, including SKU/barcode, unit, category, and applicable tax details.
  3. Use the inventory receiving or opening-stock workflow to record the starting quantities against the correct store. Use a dated opening adjustment or receipt with a reference; do not silently overwrite stock balances.
  4. Check that stock-on-hand and the inventory movement history agree for each store. If a product catalog is shared by policy, confirm which company owns each product before recording stock.

Menu names vary by enabled module and role. Only enter opening balances after quantities and valuation have been reconciled and approved by the customer.

3 · PEOPLE & ACCESS

Add users and assign least-privilege access

  1. Open Admin → Users (or the user-management page available to your role) and create a user with a unique username and an approved temporary password.
  2. Select the user's company membership and role. Assign only the permissions needed for their job.
  3. For store-scoped staff, open Store assignments for that company and assign the stores they actually use. Avoid granting access across sibling companies by default.
  4. Deliver credentials securely, require a password change when prompted, and verify that the user can see only the expected company and stores.
  5. When a staff member changes duties or leaves, disable the account or revoke the relevant membership/store assignment promptly.
A user account is a global identity; company membership, role, permissions, and store assignment determine the user's tenant access. A company transfer moves company memberships and store assignments for its members, but it does not create duplicate user identities.

4 · CONTAINER OPERATIONS

Manage company containers

  • Open Tenant containers to see active and archived boundaries, the companies they contain, and available move/archival actions.
  • Use data policies to govern whether eligible data is container-wide, company-only, store-assigned, selected-unit, or disabled. Container sharing does not merge company identities.
  • Review the subscription, invoicing, timezone, backup, and policy implications before moving a customer. Those settings and histories are container-scoped and stay with their original container.
  • Prefer archive when a container is empty. Archiving disables its active boundary while retaining billing, audit, and platform history. Hard deletion is intentionally not offered in this workflow.

5 · COMPANY TRANSFER

Move a company without merging it

  1. As a Super Admin, open Tenant containers and choose Move company.
  2. Select the active destination container and, if appropriate, a parent company. Choosing Company A as the parent places Company B alongside A while preserving B as a separate company.
  3. Run the read-only preflight. Review the stores, company memberships, per-table row counts, and any dependency blockers. No records move during preflight.
  4. Resolve blockers first. The workflow intentionally stops for cross-company product/store references, external conversation participants or file shares, membership conflicts, missing destination policies, or unsupported child-company trees.
  5. Reconcile billing and policy ownership separately. Container subscription/invoice records, backups, policies, and platform-level history are not transferred with the company. If this is the last company, cancel or suspend an active source subscription before transfer; the workflow will block auto-archiving an empty container that still has a live plan.
  6. Enter a business reason and type the exact confirmation string. The app moves the company and its supported scoped data in one transaction, checks references, records audit events, and revokes moved users' old sessions.
  7. Verify the destination company, stores, inventory, key historical records, memberships, and reports. If this was the source container's last company, that source container is archived automatically; its history remains retained.
Scope and limits: this is an in-database tenant-scope transfer, not a cross-server backup/restore. It preserves IDs and moves mapped company/store/file/message/audit data. Platform subscriptions and container configuration remain at source. If the dependency report blocks the move, do not bypass foreign-key checks or edit tenant IDs manually; resolve the ownership/reference issue and run preflight again.

FINAL VERIFICATION

Before you finish

  • Company identity, slug, parent, and destination container are correct.
  • All expected stores, products, stock balances, movements, sales, and audit history are present.
  • Company members and store assignments are correct; no duplicate destination memberships remain.
  • Cross-company data policies and product ownership have been reviewed explicitly.
  • Subscription, invoice, backup, and retention responsibilities for the archived/source container are assigned.
  • The transfer reason and generated audit trail meet the customer's change-control requirements.
